## 3. What Recruiters Look For in 2026

> *Research sources: Muzli Blog, UX Planet, Indeed Design, Skillcrush, UX Playbook*

### The Biggest Shift in 2026
**Recruiters now care more about what changed because of your work than how it looked.**  
Visuals are the entry fee — not the differentiator. Results and reasoning are.

### What Hiring Managers Prioritise (In Order)

1. **Business Impact** — Revenue increased, conversion improved, support tickets dropped. Numbers with context.
2. **User Behavior Changes** — Task completion rates, time-on-task reductions, adoption rates. Evidence that real people acted differently after your work.
3. **Process & Reasoning** — Decisions backed by reasoning, not just intuition. Why did you make this design choice? What did you test?
4. **Craft Quality** — UI polish, interactions, visual consistency. This comes 4th, not 1st.

### What Recruiters Evaluate in the First 5 Seconds
- Is the **role and specialization** visible immediately? (e.g., "UI/UX Designer specialising in FinTech")
- Are there **project thumbnails** with one-line impact descriptions?
- Is **contact information** accessible without scrolling?
- Does the page **load fast** (under 3 seconds on mobile)?
- Is there a **clear primary action** (View Work / Download Resume)?

### Red Flags That Kill Applications
- ❌ Generic tagline: "I create meaningful digital experiences" (says nothing)
- ❌ Concept projects with no real constraints or accountability
- ❌ Student projects older than 2 years
- ❌ Duplicate work showing repetition instead of range
- ❌ Projects you cannot explain conversationally
- ❌ No measurable outcomes on any case study
- ❌ Full-screen animations that load slowly
- ❌ Auto-playing audio or video
- ❌ Contact buried behind a contact page

### AI Tool Proficiency — New Filter in 2026
> **78% of design managers** consider AI tool proficiency when evaluating candidates.  
AI tool usage in your workflow must be documented in case studies.

**Tools to mention:** Figma AI, ChatGPT for research synthesis, Midjourney for moodboards.

### Niche Expertise Beats Generalism
Hiring managers increasingly prefer candidates with **demonstrated depth in a specific domain** over those who claim to do everything.  
Your domains — FinTech, HealthTech, EdTech — are strong positioning. Lead with them.

## 5. Best Font Combinations for Designer Portfolios

> *Research sources: Bestfolios, Untitled UI, Figma Resource Library, GIS User*

### Recommended Pairing (Used in Your Portfolio)

| Font | Role | Why |
|------|------|-----|
| **Syne** | Display / Headings | Architectural, geometric, used by design-forward portfolios. Memorable and distinctive. Not overused. |
| **Figtree** | Body / UI copy | Warm, rounded, highly readable. More personality than Inter/Roboto. Excellent at 16–18px. |
| **DM Mono** | Labels, tags, metadata | Clean monospace with a technical feel — perfect for section labels and code-adjacent content |

### Why This Combination Works
- **Syne** has strong visual character at display sizes — your name and section headings stand out
- **Figtree** is warm without being casual — reads well at body sizes on dark backgrounds
- **DM Mono** adds a "designer-who-understands-systems" signal without being nerdy

### Other Strong Options (For Future Redesigns)
| Heading | Body | Vibe |
|---------|------|------|
| Playfair Display | Lato | Editorial, sophisticated |
| Bricolage Grotesque | Plus Jakarta Sans | Bold, modern, energetic |
| Cabinet Grotesk | Satoshi | Creative agency feel |
| League Spartan | Source Serif | Modern classic balance |

### Typography Size Rules (Applied)
| Element | Size | Reasoning |
|---------|------|-----------|
| Hero name | `clamp(64px, 11vw, 152px)` | Dominates the page, creates impact |
| Section headings | `clamp(38px, 5.2vw, 70px)` | Scales well across screen sizes |
| Body copy | `17–18px` | WCAG recommended minimum for readability |
| Secondary text | `15px` | Comfortable for card descriptions |
| Buttons | `16px` | Interactive elements — accessibility minimum |
| Labels/tags | `11–13px` | Acceptable for short uppercase tags |
| Avoid below | `<11px` | Anything under 11px is effectively unreadable |

## 6. UI Layout Best Practices

> *Research sources: Muzli, Wegic, Casestudy.club, UXPin, IxDF*

### Layout Principles Used in Your Portfolio

**1. Max-Width Container**
All sections use `max-width: 1200px` centered — prevents content stretching on wide screens.  
Without this, text lines become too long and tiring to read.

**2. Featured Work Layout**
- ❌ Old: 3 equal columns (cramped, all projects feel the same weight)
- ✅ New: 1 full-width horizontal featured card + 2-column pair below  
This creates **visual hierarchy in the work section** — your best project gets more attention.

**3. Section Alternation**
Alternating background colors (`--bg` and `--bg-raise`) between sections creates rhythm without decorative elements. Sections feel distinct and scannable.

**4. Connected Process Steps**
A subtle horizontal line connecting the 4 process steps (01 → 02 → 03 → 04) adds flow and shows they're sequential, not independent.

**5. Hero Split Layout (Desktop)**
Left: Name, tags, description, CTAs  
Right: A live UI mockup preview (Finance Tracker dashboard in pure CSS)  
This immediately shows design skill before the visitor scrolls.

**6. Consistent 8px Spacing Grid**
All padding/margins are multiples of 8px — standard in professional UI:
- Component padding: 24px, 32px, 40px
- Section padding: 80px, 116px
- Gaps: 8px, 16px, 24px, 48px, 64px, 80px

**7. Outcome Boxes on Case Study Cards**
Every project card has a highlighted "Outcome" box — this is the first thing recruiters look for. Lime accent border makes it immediately visible.

### What Top Award-Winning Portfolios Do

| Portfolio | Key Layout Technique |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Brittany Chiang | Dark theme, sparse nav, zero decoration — work speaks for itself |
| CUSP (Awwwards SOTD) | Experimental navigation, scroll-triggered surprises |
| Griflan | Asymmetric compositions, overlapping elements |
| Carlos | Generous white space, consistent image sizing, limited color |
| Bruno Simon | Full 3D environment — unique but required unique technical skill |

**Common Pattern Across All:** 3–5 projects max, strong visual hierarchy, fast loading, mobile-responsive.

## 7. Color & Typography Trends 2026

> *Research sources: IxDF, Recursion Software, Zeenesia Studio, Tubik Blog*

### Color Trends
- **Pantone Color of the Year:** Cloud Dancer (PANTONE 11-4201) — warm off-white replacing cold corporate white
- **Dominant trend:** Neutrals-first palettes with single sharp accent colors
- **Dark mode:** Still growing — OLED-optimised deep blacks with accent colors
- **Neo-mint + pastel** pairings dominate product UI

### Your Portfolio Color Palette
| Token | Value | Role |
|-------|-------|------|
| `--bg` | `#0A0B0D` | Near-black background (OLED friendly) |
| `--bg-raise` | `#111318` | Slightly elevated sections |
| `--bg-card` | `#15181F` | Card backgrounds |
| `--text` | `#EDEBE4` | Warm off-white text (not pure white — easier on eyes) |
| `--accent` | `#C8FF4A` | Lime/acid green — distinctive, memorable, 2026-appropriate |
| `--muted` | `#8C8C96` | Secondary text |

### Why Lime Green Accent?
- Avoids the cliché purple gradient on white that most AI-generated portfolios use
- High contrast against dark background — passes WCAG AA
- Distinctive and memorable — recruiters remember it
- Signals creative confidence — not playing it safe

### Accessibility (WCAG 2.2)
- Minimum contrast for normal text: **4.5:1** (AA)
- `#C8FF4A` on `#0A0B0D` = contrast ratio **~12.5:1** ✅ (exceeds AAA)
- `#EDEBE4` on `#0A0B0D` = contrast ratio **~17:1** ✅ (exceeds AAA)

## 8. What the Best Portfolios Do Differently

> *Research sources: Casestudy.club, Designlab, Sitebuilderreport, UX Fol.io*

### Top 10 Differentiators

1. **Problem-first storytelling**  
   Lead with the user's pain, not the solution. "Users couldn't understand where their money was going" is more compelling than "I designed a finance app."

2. **Show the messy middle**  
   Wireframes, sticky notes, dead ends, pivots. Hiring managers understand how you think when they see the process, not just the polished output.

3. **Document trade-offs**  
   "We considered X but chose Y because..." This is the mark of a thoughtful designer, not a pixel pusher.

4. **Niche positioning**  
   "UI/UX Designer specialising in FinTech and HealthTech" is 10x stronger than "I create digital experiences."

5. **Metrics on every project**  
   Even estimated or qualitative metrics matter. "Reduced booking steps from 7 to 3" shows impact thinking.

6. **Personal story as a hook**  
   Your Math → Design story is genuinely unusual. It signals analytical rigour + creative thinking — exactly what product companies want.

7. **Fast, frictionless contact**  
   Email visible without scrolling. Resume downloadable in one click. No contact forms that go nowhere.

8. **Consistent personal brand**  
   Same name, photo, and bio across portfolio, LinkedIn, and Behance. Recruiters cross-reference.

9. **Mobile-first responsiveness**  
   80% of portfolio views in 2026 come from mobile. Test on a phone before sending to recruiters.

10. **AI tool transparency**  
    Mentioning how you use AI tools in your workflow (research, moodboarding, ideation) signals you're current and productive.

### Case Study Structure That Gets Interviews

A strong case study follows this arc:

```
1. THE SETUP (2–3 sentences)
   Product context | Problem statement | Business importance

2. YOUR ROLE & CONSTRAINTS (1–2 sentences)
   Specific responsibilities | Timeline | Limitations

3. PROCESS & DECISIONS (bulk of the study)
   What you tried first | What failed | Pivots | Trade-offs explained

4. OUTCOME (2–3 sentences)
   Measurable results | Qualitative signals | What changed

5. REFLECTION (1–2 sentences)
   What you'd do differently | What you learned
```

**Optimal length:** 800–1,500 words per case study with strong visual pacing.
